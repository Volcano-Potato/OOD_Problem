# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

Use the information provided below to design a rigorous empirical strategy for this anonymized applied business/economics research problem. Your goal is to produce a defensible research design, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Ground your reasoning in the background, data description, institutional details, and constraints provided in this packet.

Do not fill in packet-absent operational details, institutional features, or named design devices as if they were known facts. If multiple concrete implementations fit the packet, describe them generically or label them explicitly as illustrative examples rather than assumptions.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Public agencies and nonprofit partners often use school-based information programs to reduce risky behavior among adolescents. A persistent question is whether generic "avoid risk" messages are enough, or whether behavior changes more when young people receive more specific information about how risk differs across choices and partner types.

This matters because policymakers may overinvest in broad awareness campaigns that sound scalable but do not move the actual behavior margin that generates harm. It also matters because many studies of adolescent behavior rely on self-reported survey answers, even when those answers are highly sensitive and may not map cleanly into real downstream outcomes.

## Research Setting

Adolescents receive health-related information in a school environment before or during the period when risky behavior may begin. Some information is embedded in a standard curriculum delivered broadly across schools. Other information can be delivered through a more targeted campaign that highlights how risk varies across different types of relationships or choices. Researchers can potentially observe what information environment students faced and then follow them into a later period when objective consequences and self-reported behavior may both be measured.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether targeted risk information changes adolescent behavior more effectively than a standard generic curriculum, and to determine what kind of behavioral margin is actually affected.

## Specific Questions To Answer

1. What is the relevant causal estimand when the intervention changes the content of information rather than just whether any message is delivered?
2. How should the study compare generic risk-avoidance information with more targeted risk information?
3. What kinds of outcomes would provide credible evidence on whether behavior became safer?
4. Which parts of the design can support mechanism claims about partner choice, protection, or abstinence, and which parts cannot?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Pathway A: Targeted information may change how adolescents sort across riskier and less risky choices rather than simply whether they engage in the activity at all.
- Pathway B: More specific information may alter protection behavior even if total activity does not fall.
- Alternative explanation: Observed differences could reflect social desirability in self-reported responses rather than real behavioral change.
- Alternative explanation: A targeted campaign may work partly because of how it is delivered, not only because of what information it contains.

## Available Information

The researcher expects access to broad information on which schools or student groups received different information environments, later objective records related to a risky downstream consequence, and at least some self-reported follow-up behavior measures.

## Information Not Provided

The task packet does not provide the original source, exact sample size, exact geography, exact dates, named institutions, or a complete variable dictionary. It also does not provide the exact implementation script, the exact disease or health topic labels, or the full assignment protocol.

## Known Constraints

- The design should be feasible in a school-based adolescent information setting.
- The answer must distinguish descriptive associations, randomized or quasi-random causal claims, and mechanism claims.
- The answer should state what cannot be learned from the available information.
- Do not rely on unsupported case-specific facts that are not established in the task packet.
- Do not rely on a single polished model. Explain the identification logic, assumptions, sample restrictions, and failure modes.
