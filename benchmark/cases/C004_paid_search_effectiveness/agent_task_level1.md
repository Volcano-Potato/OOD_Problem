# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

Use the information provided below to design a rigorous empirical strategy for this anonymized applied business/economics research problem. Your goal is to produce a defensible research design, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Ground your reasoning in the background, data description, institutional details, and constraints provided in this packet.

Do not fill in packet-absent operational details, institutional features, or named design devices as if they were known facts. If multiple concrete implementations fit the packet, describe them generically or label them explicitly as illustrative examples rather than assumptions.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

A consumer-facing firm spends substantial budget on paid placements shown alongside query-driven digital traffic on a large discovery channel. Managers want to know whether these ads create genuinely incremental purchases or mostly intercept users who were already on their way to the firm through unpaid routes, direct navigation, or pre-existing familiarity.

This matters because observational return-on-ad-spend metrics can look extremely strong even when the campaign is mainly reaching already informed users. The practical decision is whether search-ad spend should be expanded, reduced, or re-targeted toward less informed user groups.

## Research Setting

The firm can influence whether sponsored placements are active for a focal class of search-driven traffic in different market areas during a live campaign. The researcher observes downstream sales outcomes over time and knows that users differ in prior familiarity, recency of activity, and likelihood of reaching the firm through other channels.

The task packet does not reveal the original firm, platform, query labels, exact market list, exact dates, or exact sample counts. The design should therefore focus on the logic of incremental demand measurement rather than on source-specific implementation details.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether paid search availability changes downstream purchases and whether any effect differs across users with different prior familiarity or activity.

## Specific Questions To Answer

The research design should answer the following, using only the information in this task packet:

1. Does making paid search available increase downstream purchases relative to a credible counterfactual?
2. How can the design distinguish truly incremental demand creation from simple interception of users who would have arrived anyway?
3. How should the researcher study whether effects differ between less active or less familiar users and already active users?
4. Which claims can be interpreted causally, and which claims should remain descriptive or conditional on stronger assumptions?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Informational discovery channel: paid search may help less familiar or less active users discover the firm and purchase.
- Interception or substitution channel: already informed users may click paid links even though they would have reached the firm through unpaid routes.
- Selection channel: observed clicks and attributed conversions may reflect underlying demand rather than ad effectiveness.
- Heterogeneity channel: average effects may look small even if certain user segments respond strongly.

## Available Information

The researcher broadly has access to campaign-on versus campaign-off conditions across multiple market areas, repeated downstream sales or purchase outcomes over time, and some information about user-history segments such as prior familiarity or prior activity intensity.

## Information Not Provided

The task packet does not provide the original source, exact sample size, exact geography, exact dates, exact query taxonomy, platform identity, or a complete variable dictionary. If your proposed design requires additional information, state it explicitly.

## Known Constraints

- The design should be feasible in the described business setting.
- The answer must distinguish descriptive associations, randomized or quasi-random causal claims, and mechanism claims.
- The answer should state what cannot be learned from the available information.
- Do not rely on unsupported case-specific facts that are not established in the task packet.
- Do not rely on a single polished model. Explain the identification logic, assumptions, sample restrictions, and failure modes.
