# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 3

## Task Rule

You must not search the web, infer the original paper, or use external literature. Use only the information provided below. Your goal is to design a rigorous empirical strategy, not to write a literature review.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Local public infrastructure projects often involve funds passing through multiple administrative layers before implementation. Monitoring may reduce leakage or misuse, but different monitoring approaches may work through different channels. Formal oversight may change the expected cost of diversion, while community participation may change local information, scrutiny, or accountability.

The outcome is also difficult to measure. Official records may not fully reveal whether resources were used as intended, and process measures such as holding meetings may not show whether corruption actually fell.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether monitoring interventions reduce corruption or resource leakage in local public projects, and to compare formal oversight with community-based monitoring where possible.

## Potential Threats

- Potential selection issue: Projects receiving monitoring may differ from unmonitored projects if assignment is related to project risk, political attention, or administrative capacity.
- Potential measurement issue: Official records may be incomplete, strategically reported, or weakly related to actual resource use and project quality.
- Potential compliance issue: Assigned monitoring or participation interventions may not be implemented as planned, and process participation may differ from actual oversight intensity.
- Potential timing issue: If monitoring is assigned after implementation choices have already been made, estimated effects may miss the relevant behavioral response.
- Potential spillover or interference: Officials, contractors, or community members may change behavior in nearby unmonitored projects after learning about monitoring elsewhere.
- Potential substitution issue: Reductions in one measured form of leakage may be offset by other unmeasured forms of corruption or quality manipulation.

## Known Constraints

- The design should match the data structure and threats above.
- The answer must distinguish causal claims from descriptive claims.
- The answer should state what cannot be learned from the available information.
- Do not assume that official spending reports alone measure corruption accurately.
- Do not rely on external facts about any named country, infrastructure program, audit institution, or prior paper.
