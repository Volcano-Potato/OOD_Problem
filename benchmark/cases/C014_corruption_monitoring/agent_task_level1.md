# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

You must not search the web, infer the original paper, or use external literature. Use only the information provided below. Your goal is to design a rigorous empirical strategy, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Treat this as an anonymous applied business/economics research problem.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Local public projects often involve multiple layers of administration, procurement, implementation, and reporting. Monitoring may reduce leakage or misuse, but different monitoring approaches can work through different channels. Formal oversight may raise the expected cost of diversion, while community participation may increase local scrutiny, information, or accountability.

The outcome is also difficult to measure. Official records may not cleanly reveal whether resources were actually used as intended, and process indicators such as meetings or complaints may not be the same thing as reduced corruption. The research task is to design an evaluation in which outcome measurement is part of identification.

## Research Setting

Local public projects receive funds, are implemented over time, and generate administrative records as well as observable physical outputs. Researchers may be able to vary monitoring intensity or participation procedures before implementation is complete, and they may be able to collect additional post-completion evidence on project quality or actual input use.

The task packet does not reveal the original country, program, project type, or detailed audit protocol. The design should therefore focus on how to create credible variation and credible outcome measurement in a corruption setting.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether monitoring interventions reduce corruption or resource leakage in local public projects, and to compare formal oversight with community-based monitoring where possible.

## Specific Questions To Answer

1. How should the study estimate the effect of monitoring on corruption or leakage?
2. What kind of outcome measure would be credible when official records may themselves be manipulable?
3. How should the design compare formal oversight with grassroots monitoring without overinterpreting process outcomes?
4. Which parts of the design support causal claims, and which forms of corruption may remain unmeasured?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Deterrence channel: formal oversight may reduce diversion by increasing the expected probability of detection or punishment.
- Participation channel: broader community involvement may improve local information or scrutiny.
- Measurement channel: official reports may be strategic, incomplete, or disconnected from true resource use.
- Substitution risk: reducing one observable form of leakage may shift behavior into other, less observable forms.

## Available Information

The researcher can observe project-level administrative information, monitoring assignments, and at least some post-implementation measures of outputs, costs, or project quality. The setting may also allow collection of community-level or project-level follow-up information.

## Information Not Provided

This task packet does not provide the original source, exact country, exact program name, exact sample size, exact audit probability, or the source paper's final outcome-construction protocol. It also does not provide a complete project engineering file.
