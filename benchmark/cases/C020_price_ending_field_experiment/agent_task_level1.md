# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

Use the information provided below to design a rigorous empirical strategy for this anonymized applied business/economics research problem. Your goal is to produce a defensible research design, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Ground your reasoning in the background, data description, institutional details, and constraints provided in this packet.

Do not fill in packet-absent operational details, institutional features, or named design devices as if they were known facts. If multiple concrete implementations fit the packet, describe them generically or label them explicitly as illustrative examples rather than assumptions.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Retailers often use prices with a salient terminal digit because they believe this format can raise demand even when the absolute price difference is small. Managers want to know whether the pricing format itself changes purchasing behavior or whether any observed demand difference mainly reflects something else, such as a low-price or promotional signal.

This is not a trivial pricing question. A demand increase under one ending format could come from psychological threshold processing, from customers inferring that the item is a bargain, or from interactions with how much information customers already have about the product.

## Research Setting

A retailer can present otherwise comparable product offers to different customer groups under different price formats. The researcher can observe which version each customer group receives and can measure downstream purchases for the focal item or offer.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether using a salient terminal-digit pricing format increases demand and to distinguish that effect from other interpretations such as generic discount signaling.

## Specific Questions To Answer

1. What is the right causal estimand: the effect of the price-ending format itself, the effect of appearing cheaper, or a broader promotional-perception effect?
2. Why is a simple comparison of items with one ending format versus another unlikely to be causal in historical pricing data?
3. What information or variation would be needed to separate the ending-format effect from sale or markdown cues?
4. How should the design handle the possibility that the effect is stronger when customers are less familiar with an item?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Pathway A: A salient terminal digit may change perception or processing of the price itself.
- Pathway B: The ending format may work mainly by signaling that the item is a bargain or discount.
- Pathway C: The effect may be stronger when customers have limited prior information about the item.
- Alternative explanation: Managers may selectively apply the pricing format to items, times, or contexts with different underlying demand.

## Available Information

The researcher expects that different customer groups can be exposed to different price formats for otherwise comparable offers, and that downstream purchase outcomes can be observed for the focal products.

## Information Not Provided

The task packet does not provide the original source, exact retailer identity, exact customer-sampling rule, exact product categories, exact item counts, or exact price values. It also does not provide the final variable dictionary or the retailer's complete pricing policy.

## Known Constraints

- The answer should state what cannot be learned from the available information.
- Do not rely on unsupported case-specific facts that are not established in the task packet.
- Do not rely on a single polished model. Explain the identification logic, assumptions, sample restrictions, and failure modes.
