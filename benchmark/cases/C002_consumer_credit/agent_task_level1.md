# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

You must not search the web, infer the original paper, or use external literature. Use only the information provided below. Your goal is to design a rigorous empirical strategy, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Treat this as an anonymous applied business/economics research problem.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

A consumer lender wants to understand why borrowers with different loan terms show different repayment outcomes. Higher borrowing costs may attract a different pool of applicants because only certain borrowers accept the offer. But higher borrowing costs may also change behavior after borrowing by changing incentives, burdens, or future consequences faced by the borrower.

These explanations matter for pricing, screening, and credit-market policy. If repayment differences mainly reflect who selects into borrowing, the lender is changing borrower composition. If they mainly reflect behavior after borrowing, the lender is changing incentives among similar borrowers. The research task is to design a study that separates these channels.

## Research Setting

The lender can make credit offers to prospective borrowers, observe whether they apply or accept, finalize loan terms, and then observe repayment outcomes. Some parts of the borrowing process can vary before the borrower decides to take up the offer, while other parts can vary only after the borrower has already entered the credit relationship.

The task packet does not reveal the original lender, exact market, exact product, or exact operational protocol. You should therefore focus on the timing and information structure that would be needed for identification.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether repayment outcomes in a consumer-credit setting are driven by borrower selection at take-up, incentive effects after borrowing, or both.

## Specific Questions To Answer

1. How should the study estimate the effect of pre-borrowing loan terms on who enters the borrower sample?
2. How can the researcher separate selection into borrowing from behavior after borrowing?
3. What information structure is needed so that later contract terms or incentives do not contaminate take-up decisions?
4. Which comparisons support causal claims about adverse selection, moral hazard, or repayment incentives, and which do not?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Selection channel: borrowers who accept one set of offer terms may differ in unobserved risk from those who reject them.
- Post-borrowing incentive channel: realized contract terms or future consequences may change repayment behavior after the loan is taken.
- Information channel: what the borrower knows at the time of application matters for whether later variation is truly post-selection.
- Interpretation risk: a single price effect on default can combine multiple mechanisms and should not be overinterpreted.

## Available Information

The researcher can observe real loan offers, application or take-up decisions, realized contract terms, and later repayment outcomes. The lender may also have pre-offer borrower characteristics, wave or branch information, and records on later credit access or repayment incentives.
