# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

Use the information provided below to design a rigorous empirical strategy for this anonymized applied business/economics research problem. Your goal is to produce a defensible research design, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Ground your reasoning in the background, data description, institutional details, and constraints provided in this packet.

Do not fill in packet-absent operational details, institutional features, or named design devices as if they were known facts. If multiple concrete implementations fit the packet, describe them generically or label them explicitly as illustrative examples rather than assumptions.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Organizations that rely on voluntary contributions often use direct personal requests to raise funds. Those requests may increase giving for very different reasons. Some people may genuinely value the social cause and appreciate the opportunity to contribute. Others may give partly because refusing a face-to-face request is uncomfortable, socially costly, or difficult to avoid in the moment.

This distinction matters for both strategy and welfare. A campaign that helps motivated contributors act on their preferences has a different interpretation from a campaign that raises money mainly by making refusal costly. The research task is to design a field study that can separate these competing explanations in a realistic fundraising environment.

## Research Setting

A fundraising organization can approach households or individuals in person. It can vary some aspects of how the solicitation is implemented before the interaction occurs, and it can observe whether contact actually happens and whether a contribution is made. The setting involves real contribution decisions rather than hypothetical survey choices.

The task packet does not reveal the original organization, exact location, exact timing, or exact implementation protocol. You should therefore focus on the logic of the design rather than on source-specific operational details.

## Research Objective

Design a study to estimate whether voluntary contributions in an in-person request setting reflect genuine willingness to support the cause, social pressure from the interaction, or both.

## Specific Questions To Answer

1. How should the study estimate the effect of solicitation conditions on whether a potential donor actually engages with the fundraiser?
2. How should the study distinguish genuine willingness to give from giving driven by discomfort with refusing a direct request?
3. What outcome patterns would support a pressure-based interpretation rather than a pure altruism or awareness interpretation?
4. Which parts of the proposed design support causal claims, and which parts would remain only suggestive?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Genuine-demand channel: some people may value the cause and actively want the opportunity to contribute.
- Pressure channel: some people may contribute mainly because avoiding or refusing a direct request is uncomfortable.
- Attention or scheduling channel: changing how the request is communicated may alter availability or awareness even without changing pressure.
- Interpretation risk: contribution amounts alone may not reveal whether observed giving is voluntary or pressured.

## Available Information

The researcher can observe a real in-person solicitation setting, whether contact occurs, and whether a contribution is made. The organization can vary some elements of the solicitation process before or during the visit. Broad operational information such as route, timing, or solicitor identity may also be available.
