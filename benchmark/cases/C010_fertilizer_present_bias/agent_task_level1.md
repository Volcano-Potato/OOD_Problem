# Anonymous Research Design Task: Level 1

## Task Rule

Use the information provided below to design a rigorous empirical strategy for this anonymized applied business/economics research problem. Your goal is to produce a defensible research design, not to write a literature review.

Do not assume that a known paper has already solved the task. Ground your reasoning in the background, data description, institutional details, and constraints provided in this packet.

Do not fill in packet-absent operational details, institutional features, or named design devices as if they were known facts. If multiple concrete implementations fit the packet, describe them generically or label them explicitly as illustrative examples rather than assumptions.

If credible causal identification is not possible from the provided information, do not invent an identification strategy. State the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational analysis instead.

## Research Background

Small producers in a low-income setting have access to a seasonal agricultural input that appears privately profitable when used in modest quantities, yet adoption remains persistently low. Policymakers and practitioners disagree on why this happens. One view is that the input is underused because producers need much larger subsidies. Another is that producers plan to invest but fail to follow through because the purchase decision is delayed until later in the season.

This matters because the policy implications are very different. If the problem is mainly low willingness to pay, then large subsidies may be justified. If the problem is instead about timing, procrastination, or small acquisition frictions at the wrong moment, then cheaper and more targeted interventions may work.

## Research Setting

Producers receive income or liquidity at one point in the production cycle and must decide whether to acquire the input either immediately or later, closer to the application date. The input can usually be purchased in small amounts, and producers are already familiar with it. The researcher wants to know whether the key barrier is static demand, liquidity, reminders, or follow-through over time.

The task packet does not reveal the original country, crop, institution, program name, exact price schedules, or exact sample size. The design should focus on the timing logic of the seasonal decision rather than on source-specific implementation details.

## Research Objective

Design a study to test whether low adoption of a profitable seasonal input is driven by timing and follow-through frictions rather than only by large price distortions or lack of awareness.

## Specific Questions To Answer

The research design should answer the following, using only the information in this task packet:

1. Does a small intervention offered early in the seasonal cycle increase actual input adoption?
2. How can the design distinguish timing and follow-through problems from simple subsidy effects?
3. What evidence would help separate procrastination or commitment problems from reminders, information, or static liquidity constraints?
4. Which claims about mechanism can and cannot be defended causally?

## Mechanism Intuition

- Follow-through channel: producers may intend to buy the input later but fail to act when the purchase moment arrives.
- Timing channel: the same economic incentive may work differently depending on whether it is offered early or late in the season.
- Static price channel: higher adoption could simply reflect a stronger subsidy rather than a timing effect.
- Salience or reminder channel: some apparent timing effects could instead reflect reminders or temporary attention shifts.

## Available Information

The researcher has access to a seasonal field setting, observed adoption or use of the input, and the ability to vary when small offers, ordering opportunities, or other purchase conditions are presented during the cycle.

## Information Not Provided

The task packet does not provide the original source, exact geography, exact dates, exact subsidy values, named organizations, or a complete variable dictionary. If your proposed design requires additional information, state it explicitly.
