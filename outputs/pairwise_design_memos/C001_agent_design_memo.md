The central identification challenge is partial observability with endogenous selection. Giving outcomes Gᵢ and Yᵢ are observed only when contact occurs (Cᵢ = 1). The treatment Tᵢ can affect both the probability of contact and the composition of the contacted pool. ## Proposed Design
### Design Architecture Randomly assign each planned contact opportunity i to one of three conditions: - Arm A (Baseline/No Notice): Fundraiser visits without prior communication. This is the standard unannounced door-to-door solicitation. - Arm B (Notice Only): One to three days before the planned visit, the household receives advance notice (e.g., a flyer, postcard, or door hanger) indicating that a fundraiser will visit during a specified time window. No explicit opt-out mechanism is provided. - Arm C (Notice + Opt-Out): Same advance notice as Arm B, but the notice includes a low-cost way to decline the in-person visit. The opt-out could be: check a box and leave the notice on the door, send a brief text message to a designated number, or check a box on a simple web form. ## What Cannot Be Claimed
Based on the available information, the following claims are not supported: 1. "Social pressure causes X% of all giving." The design can estimate the share of giving that disappears when avoidance is made easy, but this is the combined effect of selection (who avoids) and behavioral response (pressure on those who remain). Bounds can be placed on the behavioral component, but point identification requires untestable assumptions. 2. "Genuine willingness is the only motive for giving." Even if conditional giving is identical across all arms, this does not rule out pressure. Pressure may operate uniformly across all contacted units, or it may be offset by other differences between arms. 3. "The opt-out mechanism reveals true preferences."
