# Agent Design Memo

## Research Question
What is the causal effect of actual digital ad exposure on downstream user conversions (visits, registrations, purchases, or revenue), in a setting where the platform's delivery algorithm selects which eligible users receive impressions? Refined sub-questions: - What is the effect of being assigned or made eligible for the campaign (the ITT)? - What is the effect of actually receiving the focal ad impression, for users whose exposure status responds to assignment? - How much of any observed association between exposure and conversions is attributable to platform selection rather than to the ad itself? ---

## Target Estimand
### Primary Causal Estimand Intention-to-Treat (ITT): The average effect of campaign assignment (or eligibility) on the downstream conversion outcome, over all users assigned or made eligible. \[ \tau_{\text{ITT}} = E[Y_i(1) - Y_i(0) \mid \text{assigned/eligible}] \] where \(Y_i(1)\) is the potential outcome under assignment and \(Y_i(0)\) is the potential outcome under non-assignment. This estimand is directly identified from the randomization or holdout structure described in Stage 2. ## Identification Logic
The central challenge is that the platform delivery algorithm creates a non-ignorable selection process between assignment and actual exposure. Yes, credible causal identification is possible for two estimands: 1. ## What Cannot Be Claimed
1. "The ad causes a X% lift in conversions for all users." The CACE applies only to compliers. The ATE and ATT are not identified. 2. "The treatment effect is the same for heavy users and light users." Heterogeneity is estimable only for the ITT, and ITT heterogeneity confounds compliance heterogeneity with effect heterogeneity. 3. "Each additional impression increases conversions by Y%." The binary exposure design does not identify the marginal effect of incremental impressions. A dose-response claim requires additional modeling assumptions. 4. "The ad is cost-effective with ROI of Z%." This requires cost data (not in the packet) and an ATE or ATT estimate (not identified). The CACE for compliers can inform but not fully answer ROI questions. 5. "There is no spillover or interference." The packet explicitly flags possible spillover.
