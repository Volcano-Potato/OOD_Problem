# Agent Design Memo

## Research Question
In a consumer-credit setting where borrowers receive offers, decide whether to take up a loan, and later enter repayment, to what extent are repayment outcomes driven by (a) the types of borrowers who select into borrowing under different pre-borrowing terms, versus (b) the causal effect of realized post-borrowing contract terms and repayment-linked incentives on borrower behavior? ---

## Identification Logic
The fundamental challenge is sample selection on unobservables interacting with treatment. Specifically: 1. Selection into the repayment sample is endogenous. Only borrowers who accept a loan enter Stage 3 where Y_repay is observed. The decision to accept depends on Z_pre and on borrower characteristics (observed and unobserved). If we compare Y_repay across Z_pre arms without adjustment, we cannot separate "Z_pre caused different types of people to borrow" (M1) from "Z_pre caused different repayment behavior conditional on borrowing" (M2, if Z_pre flows through to contract terms). 2. Z_post is only defined for accepters. The post-borrowing incentive treatment cannot be randomized across the full offer sample because it only applies to loans that exist. ## Defensibility Assessment
Yes, credible causal identification is possible — if and only if the following conditions are jointly satisfied: 1. The lender has the operational ability to independently randomize both Z_pre (across all eligible borrowers) and Z_post (across accepted borrowers). 2. Z_post is not revealed, signaled, or predictable by prospective borrowers at the take-up stage. 3. The randomization mechanisms are not compromised by staff discretion, borrower communication, or operational leakage. 4. Attrition between acceptance and repayment observation is minimal and uncorrelated with treatment assignment. If any of these conditions fail, a credible causal decomposition is not possible, and the analysis should revert to the strongest defensible descriptive or correlational strategy (see Section 9, Alternative). ---

## Proposed Design
### Primary Design: Two-Stage Randomized Experiment with Information Separation Stage 1: Pre-Borrowing Randomization (Z_pre). From the pool of eligible prospective borrowers, randomly assign offer terms Z_pre ∈ {z₁, z₂, ..., z_K} at the borrower-offer level. Z_pre must include terms that are salient to the take-up decision (e.g., offered interest rate, loan amount ceiling). Randomization should be stratified on pre-offer risk measures to improve precision. Stage 2: Post-Acceptance Randomization (Z_post). Among borrowers who accept a loan, randomly assign post-acceptance contract terms or repayment-linked incentives Z_post ∈ {w₁, w₂, ..., w_L}. Crucially, Z_post must be independent of Z_pre (orthogonal assignment). The randomization of Z_post must occur after acceptance, and borrowers must not have learned Z_post beforehand. Information Protocol. Borrowers must receive Stage 1 offer terms without any information about Stage 2 terms. The offer communication must not reference, hint at, or allow inference about post-acceptance terms. A post-acceptance disclosure provides Z_post after the contract is executed. ### Assignment Structure Eligible Borrowers (N) │ ▼ Randomize Z_pre ──────────────────────────────┐ │ │ ├──► Accept (N_A) ──► Randomize Z_post ─┤ │ │ │ │ └──► Observe Y_repay │

## What Cannot Be Claimed
1. General equilibrium effects. The design estimates partial-equilibrium effects within one lender's portfolio. Market-level effects (e.g., borrower reallocation across lenders, aggregate credit supply changes) are not identified. 2. Long-run welfare effects. The design identifies repayment behavior over the observation window. Whether observed repayment changes reflect improved borrower welfare or merely delayed default is not assessable without a welfare model and longer follow-up. 3. External validity to other lenders, markets, or borrower populations. The estimates are local to the lender's borrower population, product features, and institutional context. 4. Causal claims about individual borrower characteristics as drivers of selection. The design identifies that Z_pre causes compositional shifts. It does not identify why specific borrower types are more responsive to specific terms
