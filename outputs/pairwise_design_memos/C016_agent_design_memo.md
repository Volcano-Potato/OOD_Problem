## Research Question
Does providing adolescents with targeted information about how risk varies across partner types or relationship choices produce different behavioral outcomes — in level, composition, or protection behavior — compared with exposure only to a standard generic risk-avoidance curriculum? Ancillary questions: - Does any effect of targeted information operate through safer partner selection, increased protective behavior within relationships, or reduced overall activity? - Can changes in self-reported behavior be distinguished from changes in reporting norms or comfort? ---

## Identification Logic
The central identification challenge is selection into treatment at the school level. Schools are not randomly assigned to the targeted-information condition. The design described in the packet is one of "planned school-level variation," not necessarily random assignment. Schools that receive the targeted campaign may differ systematically from those that receive only the generic curriculum — in baseline risk levels, administrative capacity, community norms, prior health-program participation, or unobserved student and parent characteristics that correlate with both treatment assignment and outcomes. Without randomization, the naive comparison of objective outcomes across information conditions conflates the causal effect of the information content with pre-existing differences between the schools that were selected (or self-selected) into each condition. ## What Cannot Be Claimed
1. Causal effects cannot be claimed unless random assignment or another credible identification device is confirmed. The packet provides "planned school-level variation" but not randomization. 2. General equilibrium effects cannot be identified. The design measures individual-level outcomes within an information environment but cannot capture how the aggregate shift in partner choices reshapes the risk landscape for all students. The estimated effect is a partial-equilibrium effect that may not generalize to settings where the intervention is scaled to the entire population. 3. The specific mechanism cannot be definitively isolated.
